Samantha helps you turn ideas, work, and notes into a simple content plan you can actually follow, with reminders to keep things moving.

## **Best for**

- founders, marketers, and creators managing content alongside other work
- people who post inconsistently because planning takes too much effort
- teams that want a clearer content rhythm
- anyone who wants less guesswork and better consistency

## **What you’ll get**

- a weekly or monthly content plan built around your goals
- content ideas pulled from your work, notes, and conversations
- a clearer publishing rhythm across channels
- reminders for drafting, review, and publishing
- a running backlog of ideas for future content

## **How content calendar planning works**

### **Step 1: Set your content rhythm**

Start by giving Samantha the basic context for your content.

**For example:**

```text

Help me plan content for next week.

I post on LinkedIn and Twitter 3 times a week.

My audience is busy professionals.
```

Samantha can help organize around:

- your content channels
- posting frequency
- audience
- themes or goals
- upcoming topics you want to cover

### **Step 2: Turn your work into content ideas**

If you already have notes, meetings, or ideas, Samantha can help turn them into content topics.

**For example:**

```text

Generate 10 content ideas based on what I work on.

Turn my meeting notes from today into a LinkedIn post idea.

What topics have I not covered in a while?
```

This helps you create content from real work instead of starting from zero every time.

### **Step 3: Build the calendar**

Once you have topics, Samantha can help spread them across a realistic schedule.

**For example:**

```text

Plan my content for next week with 3 LinkedIn posts and 2 tweets.

Give me a month of content ideas for a productivity-focused audience.

Space these topics across the month.
```

This helps you avoid bunching everything together or deciding day by day.

### **Step 4: Draft and prepare content**

Once the plan is in place, Samantha can help you move from topic to draft.

**For example:**

```text

Draft the LinkedIn post for Monday based on the daily planning topic.

Write a short post about how I use Samantha for weekly reviews.

Turn this note into a post draft.
```

This makes the calendar more useful by helping you actually create what is planned.

### **Step 5: Stay consistent with reminders**

Planning only helps if the content actually gets written and posted.

**For example:**

```text

Remind me to review and schedule all posts every Sunday evening.

What content am I supposed to post this week?

Remind me to write my weekly newsletter every Wednesday morning.
```

This helps you keep momentum without relying on memory.

### **Example prompts**

```text

Plan my content for next week: 3 LinkedIn posts and 2 tweets.

Generate 10 content ideas based on what I work on.

Draft a LinkedIn post about how I use Samantha for daily planning.

What content am I supposed to post this week?

Remind me to write my weekly newsletter every Wednesday morning.

Turn my meeting notes from today into a LinkedIn post.

Give me a month of content ideas for a productivity-focused audience.

What topics have I not covered in a while that I should revisit?
```

## **Tips for better results**

<Tip>
  - plan content in batches instead of deciding every day
  - turn meetings, decisions, and wins into content ideas
  - keep a running backlog of topics
  - set reminders for drafting and publishing, not just brainstorming
  - revisit older themes so your content stays consistent without getting repetitive
</Tip>
