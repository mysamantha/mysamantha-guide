---
title: "Task Capture + Reminders"
---

Tasks get buried in email threads, meeting notes, and scattered thoughts. Samantha helps you capture them quickly, organize them clearly, and remind you at the right time.

Instead of keeping tasks in different places and hoping you remember them later, you can turn everything into one clear, up-to-date task system.

## **Best for**

- Busy professionals managing tasks across meetings, email, and day-to-day work
- People who often remember things too late
- Anyone who wants fewer mental reminders and a more reliable system
- Users who need help turning messy input into clear next steps

## **What you’ll get**

- Tasks captured from notes, emails, and conversations
- Reminders set for the right time
- Clearer deadlines and priorities
- One place to review what is due, overdue, or still unscheduled
- Less risk of things slipping through the cracks

## **How task capture and reminders work**

### **Step 1: Capture tasks fast**

You can give Samantha tasks in whatever form they show up.

**For example:**

```text

Add a task to follow up with the design team.

Remind me to send the proposal to Acme by Thursday at 4 PM.

Extract all action items from these meeting notes.
```

You do not need to clean things up first. Samantha can work from rough input.

### **Step 2: Turn messy input into structured tasks**

If your tasks are buried inside notes or a long message, Samantha can pull them out and organize them.

She can help by:

- extracting individual action items
- identifying deadlines
- setting priorities
- spotting tasks that need follow-up
- turning one messy note into a usable task list

This is useful when tasks come from meetings, email threads, or brain dumps.

### **Step 3: Add reminders that actually help**

Once a task is captured, Samantha can help make sure it shows up at the right time.

For example:

```text

Remind me to submit my expense report every Friday at 4 PM.

Remind me about this tomorrow morning.

Set a recurring task for standup prep every Monday at 8:45 AM
```

This helps you rely less on memory and more on a system that stays current.

### **Step 4: Review what needs attention**

You can also ask Samantha to help you see what needs action now.

For example:

```text

What tasks are due this week?

Show me everything due today.

What’s overdue?

Which tasks have no deadline?
```

This makes it easier to stay on top of your workload without digging through everything manually.

### **Step 5: Adjust when plans change**

If priorities shift, Samantha can help you update tasks quickly.

**For example:**

```text

Move the Acme deadline to Friday.

Move everything due today that I haven’t started to tomorrow morning.

Help me set deadlines for tasks that don’t have one
```

This helps keep your task list realistic instead of outdated.

### **Example prompts**

```text

Add a task to follow up with the design team about the homepage.

Extract all action items from these meeting notes.

Remind me to submit my expense report every Friday at 4 PM.

What tasks are due this week? Sort by priority.

Move everything due today that I haven’t started to tomorrow morning.

Which tasks have no deadline? Help me set one for each.

Create a recurring task for standup prep every Monday at 8:45 AM.

Flag any tasks I’ve had open for more than 7 days
```

### **Tips for better results**

<Tip>
  - Paste messy notes directly instead of filtering them first
  - Include a rough deadline, even if it is just “this week”
  - Review overdue tasks regularly
  - Use recurring reminders for repeated responsibilities
  - Ask Samantha to find tasks with no deadline before they get ignored
</Tip>
